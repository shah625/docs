**Boost** is a switch on the interval ticket. A regular interval contract pays the same $1 a contract whether the price closes one cent or a hundred dollars past the price to beat. A **Boost order** pairs that contract with an **option leg** whose strike is the **live price the moment you place the order**. Every dollar the price moves your way from there adds to the payout, up to **100× your stake**.

<Warning>
A Boost order is a real-money order like any other. If the price closes on the wrong side of the price to beat, you can lose your whole stake. The option leg pays only for the distance the price closes past its strike, the live price when you placed the order.
</Warning>

## How it's built

Your stake buys two things:

| Part | What it is |
|------|------------|
| **The contract** | The regular Up / Down contract. Enough of your stake buys it that a right call pays your **whole stake back**. Example: $10 on Up when Up costs 65¢ puts $6.50 into 10 contracts, which pay $10 on any close at or above the price to beat. |
| **The option leg** | The rest of your stake ($3.50 in the example) buys a **call** on Up or a **put** on Down. Its **strike** is the live price when your order is placed. At the close it pays a fixed amount for every $1 the asset finishes past the strike (above it for a call, below it for a put), up to a **ceiling**. Because it stops adding at the ceiling, it's a call spread or put spread. |

The strike and the ceiling are fixed when you buy, so the most an order can pay is known up front: **100× your stake**, or what the contract alone pays if that's more.

## How it pays

At the close, a Boost order pays the sum of its two parts:

- **The contract:** your stake, if the close is on your side of the price to beat. Otherwise nothing.
- **The option leg:** a fixed amount for every $1 the close is past your strike, up to the ceiling. Otherwise nothing.

The strike is the price you bought at, not the price to beat, so what you get depends on which side was winning when you bought.

**Bought while your side was winning.** Your strike is already past the price to beat. You're in profit from the first cent the price closes past where you bought.

| Where the price closes | What the order pays |
|------------------------|---------------------|
| On the wrong side of the price to beat | $0. You lose your stake. |
| On your side, but not past your strike | Your **stake back** (the contract pays, the option leg doesn't) |
| Past your strike | Your stake back **plus** the option leg's payout for every $1 past the strike |
| At or past the ceiling | The most it can pay |

**Bought while your side was losing.** Your strike sits on the losing side of the price to beat. The option leg gains from the first move your way, but the order makes a profit only once the price closes past the price to beat.

| Where the price closes | What the order pays |
|------------------------|---------------------|
| Short of your strike | $0. You lose your stake. |
| Between your strike and the price to beat | Only the option leg pays: part of your stake back |
| At or past the price to beat | Your stake back **plus** the option leg's payout for the whole distance from your strike |
| At or past the ceiling | The most it can pay |

- An Up order wins on a close at or above the price to beat. A Down order loses on an unchanged close, because an unchanged close settles Up. See the [tie rule](/interval/how-windows-work#up-and-down).
- The gold figure on the ticket is the option leg's rate. If it reads "+$23.26 · every $100 BTC climbs" and BTC closes $200 past your strike on your side of the price to beat, the order pays your stake back plus about $46.52.

## How the option leg is quoted

Boost is new, and its option legs don't trade on an order book. There's no live book of bids and asks for them, so the market's **Book** tab shows the regular contract only. Instead the option leg is quoted on request (**RFQ**, request for quote): a price is worked out for your order at the moment you place it, from the asset's live price and how far it's expected to move in the time left.

- The contract part is priced at the regular contract's current ask on the market's order book.
- A cash-out is quoted the same way, at the moment you tap it: the contract at the book's current bid, plus what the option leg is worth then.
- Each quote is made for your order at that moment, so the gold figure and the Boost card can change between when you look and when you buy. If the payout moves more than 10% against what the ticket showed, the order is refused so you can check the new figures.

## Where it's offered

Boost is offered on the **5-, 15- and 60-minute** stock, crypto and Pre-IPO markets. It isn't offered on daily markets or on Gold and Silver.

- The **Boost** row sits under the amount on the **Buy** ticket. It isn't on **Limit**.
- Boost and ⚡ **1-tap** are one or the other: turning Boost on turns 1-tap off.
- When a side is priced so close to $1 that almost nothing is left for the option leg, the Boost row hides. It comes back when the price does.
- Trade credit is used before your cash, as on a regular buy. Under the **Buy** button the ticket shows the split, for example "Uses $3.00 credit + $2.00 cash · you keep the profit". What happens to the credit at the close or on a sale is on the [Trade credit](/portfolio/trade-credit#boost-orders) page.
- You need **Allow trading** first.

### When it's open

Boost is open whenever the market is, in every window, until the window's **last minute**. That includes the stock market's open and close, the US data releases, and every session a stock trades in (the weekend too, for the stocks that trade through it). Crypto and Pre-IPO markets offer it around the clock.

The last-minute cutoff means you can buy in the first 4 minutes of a 5-minute window, the first 14 of a 15-minute window, and the first 59 of a 60-minute window. In the last minute the Buy button reads **Closed for the last 1 minute**, and the note under it reads "Switch Boost off to trade Up / Down."

## Placing a Boost order

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the ticket">
    Open a stock, crypto or Pre-IPO market, or tap **Up** or **Down** on a market card. Keep the ticket on **Buy**, and pick **▲ Up** or **▼ Down**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your stake">
    Type an amount, or use the chips. A Boost order is **$1 to $100**. Once you've typed an amount, the Boost row shows the option leg's rate in gold, for example "+$23.26 · every $100 BTC climbs". Before you type one, it reads **Win More**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on Boost">
    Flip the switch on the **Boost** row. The **Boost card** opens above the Buy button (see below). Tap the ⓘ beside **Boost** for a short explainer, **How Boost works**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buy">
    Tap **Buy Up Boost $10** (or Down). The option leg is struck at the live price as your order is placed, and your stake leaves your cash at once (any trade credit is used first). The **Trade executed** card reads "Bought $10.00 Up Boosted" and "Wins up to $1,000.00 · settles 3:15 PM". You can add a take or skip it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
A **NEW** tag sits beside **Boost** until December 1, 2026.
</Note>

## Reading the Boost card

The card shows the real numbers for the amount you typed, and they move with the price.

| Part | What it says |
|------|--------------|
| **The big gold figure** | What the option leg adds for each step the asset moves your way past your strike, for example **+$23.26** "every $100 BTC climbs" (or "falls" for Down). The step is a round amount picked for the window, so it doesn't change as the clock runs down. |
| **If it ended now** | Shown while your side is winning: where the price stands against the price to beat ("BTC is $412.50 above the price to beat") and what the order would pay if the window ended now: your stake back, for example "$10.00 back". Your strike would be the price right now, so the option leg adds nothing yet. |
| **Get your money back** | Shown while your side is losing: how far the asset has to move your way for the order to pay your stake back, for example "if BTC climbs $120.00". |

<Note>
On a phone the trade dialog pins **You risk** and the gold figure above the Buy button, with the **Boost** row.
</Note>

## After you buy

- **On the chart**, a gold line marks your option leg's strike, **"Boost from $X"**, until the window closes.
- **Live Positions** on the market page lists your open Boost orders above your regular rows: the side, "$10.00 stake", and a **Cash out ~$X** button. The line under it reads "Now ~$10.40 (+$0.40) · wins up to $1,000.00", or "wins up to $1,000.00 · pays off the close price" when there's no live value. It shows **Open** while the window runs, **Paying…** at the close, and **Selling…** while a cash-out goes through. If trade credit funded part of the stake, the row says how much, for example "· $2.00 credit".
- **Your Portfolio** lists it among your interval positions as "Boost $10.00 stake", and its Activity rows carry a **Boost** stamp. See [Positions and history](/portfolio/positions-and-history).
- **On the feed**, a Boost buy carries a shimmering gold **Boost** tag. See [Profiles and social](/platform/profiles-and-social).

## Cashing out early

You can sell an open Boost order back before the window's last minute:

1. Tap **Cash out ~$X** on its row in **Live Positions**. The figure is what reaches your cash now. The button reads **Selling…** while it goes through.
2. The money goes to your cash straight away, and you get a notification that reads, for example, "Sold for $6.12 (+$1.12)".

- The cash-out price is **below the order's live value** ("Now ~$X"), so buying and cashing straight back out costs money.
- **Cash out** shows while there's a fresh price and the order is worth at least 1¢ to sell. It disappears in the window's last minute, the same cutoff as buying.
- If the price drops more than 10% below the one shown as you tap, the sale is refused so you can check the new price.
- If trade credit funded part of the stake, **Cash out ~$X** shows what reaches your cash. A sale at a loss refills your credit first, so the button can read "~$2.00 credit" when the whole sale goes back to your credit. Hover it for the split. See [Trade credit](/portfolio/trade-credit#boost-orders).

If a sale can't go through, you see a message such as "This order has nothing to sell right now — it still pays off at the close." or "We couldn't pay the sale — nothing moved and your order is still open."

## At the close

A Boost order settles automatically on the window's result, like any interval contract: the contract pays if the close is on your side of the price to beat, and the option leg pays for the distance past its strike. The payout goes to your cash shortly after the window's result is in. If a result still can't be read 6 hours after the close, your stake is refunded.

You get a notification in the **Trade results** category, linked to the settled window's page. Its title names the market and the side, for example "Bitcoin (BTC) · 15 min · Boosted Up". The text under it says how the order did:

| Outcome | Notification text |
|---------|-------------------|
| Paid out more than the stake | "You won $7.40 · paid $17.40" (the profit, then what was paid) |
| Paid back less than the stake | "You lost $3.00 · paid $7.00" |
| Settled worthless | "You lost $10.00" |
| Paid back exactly the stake | "Broke even · paid $10.00" |
| Refunded | "Refunded $10.00 · no close price" |
| Cashed out early | "Sold for $6.12 (+$1.12)" |

If trade credit funded part of the stake, the text counts your cash result and says what happened to the credit.

As a push notification on your phone, a win reads "You won $7.40" over the market. Anything else names the market and says "Your trade settled. Tap to see how it went."

The settled window's page shows the window's result, not your order's payout. Your Portfolio **Activity** has a **Buy** row for the order and one result row (**Sell**, **Won**, **Lost** or **Settled**), each with the **Boost** stamp.

## Fees and stats

- There's no trading fee on a Boost order. The option leg has a price, like any option, and the figures on the ticket already include it.
- Your **Portfolio** value counts open Boost orders at their live value, or at the stake when there's none.
- A Boost order's result, what it paid less its stake, counts toward your **Realized P&L** and the leaderboards like any other trade.

## Limits and messages

| Rule | What you see |
|------|--------------|
| **Stake** $1 to $100 per order | The button reads **Min $1** or **Max $100 per order**. If refused: "Enter between $1 and $100." |
| **No new orders in a window's last minute** | **Closed for the last 1 minute**, and "Switch Boost off to trade Up / Down." |
| **The price moved** more than 10% against what the ticket showed | "The price moved — check the new payout and try again." |
| **No fresh price** yet | "Waiting for a fresh price — try again in a moment." |
| **No price** for your side | "No price for this side right now." |
| **The window isn't trading** | "This window isn't trading right now." |
| **Not enough cash** (cash plus trade credit) | "Not enough cash or trade credit for this stake." |
| **Trade credit can't be read** right now | "Trade credit is unavailable right now — try again in a moment." |
| **Allow trading** not done yet | "Allow trading first, then place the order again." |
| **An earlier order** is still confirming | "Your order is being confirmed — check your orders in a moment before trying again." |
| **The market doesn't offer it** | The Boost row doesn't show. |

The limits apply to Boost orders only. Regular interval buys aren't affected.
